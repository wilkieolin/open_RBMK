Restructuring the repository on 24 September exposed a silent substitution. DRAGON resolves its nuclear data through a hook that decompresses the library in place inside the libraries submodule — and on every later run finds the decompressed file already sitting there and uses it without re-checking. When the submodule moved to a newer library build, the leftover from the previous one kept winning.

The reference cell returns 1.310172 on the library that was actually used and 1.308679 on the one the repository ships: a systematic −87 pcm under every DRAGON figure below. The cache is content-addressed now, so it cannot recur; the figures have not yet been re-measured. The OpenMC column is unaffected — it is continuous-energy ENDF/B-VIII.1 and never reads a draglib — so the DRAGON/OpenMC bracket has to be recomputed rather than relabelled.

Orientation

Reading the numbers

Everything below is a statement about reactivity — how far a reactor is from steady self-sustaining operation, and in which direction. Three units carry that, and they appear on every chart.

k
The multiplication factor: neutrons in one fission generation divided by neutrons in the one before. k = 1 is steady power. k∞ ("k-infinity") is that ratio for a single fuel cell with no leakage out of the sides; keff is the real, finite reactor.
ρ, pcm
Reactivity, ρ = 1 − 1/k, measured in pcm — pour cent mille, parts per hundred thousand. 100 pcm = 0.1 % change in k. A change in reactivity is always quoted relative to some reference state; here that reference is always the same fuel at the same burnup with the coolant at its normal density.
β
The delayed-neutron fraction. Roughly 0.5 % of fission neutrons are not emitted promptly but seconds to minutes later, by the decay of fission products. Those stragglers are the entire reason a reactor can be controlled by mechanical rods: they stretch the response time from microseconds to seconds.
$ (dollar)
Reactivity divided by β. One dollar is the threshold at which a reactor is critical on prompt neutrons alone — the delayed ones are no longer needed, the stretched response time collapses, and power runs away in milliseconds. Below $1 a reactor is controllable; above it, nothing mechanical is fast enough. This is the number that matters, and it is why β had to be measured rather than assumed.

Two more terms recur:

void coefficient
How reactivity changes as coolant water boils away. In most reactors water is the moderator, so losing it shuts the reaction down — the coefficient is negative and the reactor is self-stabilising. In an RBMK the moderator is graphite (89.6 % of the cell by volume); the water is mainly a neutron absorber. Boiling it off therefore adds reactivity. That positive coefficient is the physics at the centre of this project.
burnup
How much energy a unit mass of fuel has produced, in megawatt-days per tonne (MWd/t). It stands in for how far the fuel's composition has evolved: U-235 consumed, plutonium bred in, fission products accumulated. Chernobyl's core was at high burnup, and nearly every dangerous property below gets worse with it.
A3 · foundation

One bad isotope label

The lattice cell describes one fuel channel: 18 fuel rods in two rings, a zirconium pressure tube, and the graphite block around it. Every material composition for the whole project comes from one shared procedure, so the cell deck and the branch deck cannot drift apart.

That procedure declared the coolant's hydrogen as H1 — free-gas hydrogen, an unbound proton. Hydrogen in liquid water is not free: it is bound into a molecule whose vibrational and rotational modes change how it scatters slow neutrons. The correct label is H1_H2O.

Free-gas hydrogen overstates water's moderating power, which makes the water look more useful than it is — and therefore makes boiling it away look less rewarding. The fix changed k∞ by only 264 pcm, but it changed the full-void worth from +82 pcm to +222 pcm: it nearly tripled the quantity the whole project exists to get right.

processA fix that lives in a copy of the file is not a fix

The correction had in fact been made a day earlier — in a copy of the procedure, RbmkLibH.c2m. Six diagnostic decks were pointed at the copy and reported the right answer, while the two headline decks still called the original and ran the wrong composition. The copy has been deleted and the callers repointed. This is now a standing rule in the project log.

A5b · the structural gap

Connecting the cell to the core

Until now nothing in the project had ever written cross sections to disk. Every good cell calculation left its result in memory and discarded it at exit, so the whole-core solver ran on generic textbook constants for a pressurised-water reactor — a different reactor with the opposite sign of void coefficient. The lattice work and the core work were not connected at all.

MULTICOMPO
A reactivity database. The detailed transport calculation is run once per combination of conditions, condensed from 172 neutron energy groups down to 2, and tabulated. The core solver then interpolates it instead of re-running transport — which it could never afford to do 1661 times per time step.

The database now spans four axes — burnup, coolant density, fuel temperature and graphite temperature — at every combination: 630 points, 81 minutes, 114 MB. Each point is a full re-solve including re-computed self-shielding, which is not optional: boiling the coolant hardens the neutron spectrum into exactly the uranium resonances that self-shielding governs.

But "a database exists on disk" is not evidence of anything. Three checks:

630branch points, all present, no non-convergence
6.4 pcmworst round-trip loss, 8 nodes spanning every axis extreme
27 pcmworst off-grid interpolation error — what the transient will actually use
−0.5 %β against an independent Monte Carlo measurement

The round trip compares the detailed 172-group transport answer against what comes back out of the database after condensation, homogenisation, storage and interpolation. Losing 6.4 pcm through all of that is two orders of magnitude below the physics uncertainties here. The off-grid test matters more: the transient will never land exactly on a tabulated point, so it measures error at the midpoints of the coolant-density and temperature axes. Burnup is deliberately left on grid nodes — fuel composition does not change over the forty seconds of an accident, while the other three swing violently.

An earlier check on a single corner point had reported 0.4 pcm. That was the best case by a factor of sixteen; the honest number is 6.4.

A5b · delayed neutrons

β falls as the fuel ages

The project had been assuming β = 0.0048 throughout. β is not a constant: it is a property of which nuclide is fissioning. Uranium-235 emits delayed neutrons at 0.0065 of its fissions; the plutonium-239 that breeds in as fuel ages emits them at only 0.0023. As plutonium takes over, β falls.

Both figures were read straight out of the nuclear data library rather than quoted — and the resulting curve drops 31 % over the fuel's life.

The cross-check is worth stating carefully, because the two methods share almost nothing. DRAGON computes a flux-weighted ratio of delayed to total fission production in 172 energy groups on a deterministic transport solve. OpenMC runs the same cell twice in continuous energy by Monte Carlo — once with delayed neutrons enabled and once without — and takes βeff = 1 − kprompt/ktotal. They agree to 0.5 %, inside the Monte Carlo error bar.

near missA wrong answer that was self-consistent

The first extraction returned 0.006524 at every burnup, every void fraction and every temperature — identical to six digits. Two independent mistakes produced it: the database query GETVAL 'NUSIGF' 1 takes a start index, not a count, and the aged fuel's data is stored as 18 separate fissioning nuclides. So element 1 was uranium-235 in both the numerator and the denominator, at every state — and 0.006524 is exactly uranium-235's β.

Being self-consistent across ten different states made it look more trustworthy, not less. What caught it was knowing beforehand what the physics had to do: β must fall, so a flat curve is a defect to explain, not a result to record.

A5a · the central quantity

The void coefficient, and a disagreement

Two independent codes now compute the same quantity: how much reactivity is added when the coolant in a fuel channel boils away completely. Both agree it is positive and that it rises steeply with burnup. That is the RBMK's defining hazard reproduced from first principles, by two methods, with no tuning.

They disagree on the magnitude by about a factor of two.

Until today the comparison had only two coolant densities — full and empty — so the disagreement was a single number with no structure. Adding two intermediate densities to the Monte Carlo side changed the picture, and the change is the most useful result of the day.

The ratio between the two codes at discharge burnup is 1.95 ± 0.14, 1.98 ± 0.09 and 1.83 ± 0.05 at the three measured void fractions — constant within its own error bars. So rather than pick a winner, the project carries both and lets the accident simulation decide whether the difference changes the outcome.

At low burnup the ratio is meaningless and must not be used: DRAGON's own void worth dips to +155 pcm at 5 MWd/t, so the ratio is a small number divided by a smaller one, and at fresh fuel the two curves disagree in shape rather than scale. Neither is the state the reactor was in.

Result

Why it mattered that the fuel was old

Converting reactivity to dollars combines the two results above, and the two effects compound: the numerator rises steeply with burnup while β, the denominator, falls. The danger grows twice over.

From fresh fuel to discharge the worth of complete voiding grows 14× (DRAGON) or 5.7× (OpenMC) in dollars. At discharge burnup it is +4.7 $ and +8.6 $ — prompt-supercritical at both ends of the bracket. This is the first quantitative statement the project can make about why the reactor was dangerous, and it does not depend on resolving the factor of two.

caveatThis is not yet the accident

What is plotted is the equilibrium worth of completely voiding a channel at fixed burnup — not a transient, not the real core, and not the graphite-tipped control rods that turned the scram itself into an insertion of positive reactivity. Those come next, and the reference transient will be run under both ends of the bracket.

A2 · whole core

The core geometry solves, and quickly

The 3-D core deck — 49 × 49 × 32 nodes, 12.25 × 12.25 × 8 m, 76 832 regions — now runs and converges, at keff = 1.123062. That value is not a physics result: it still uses the placeholder textbook constants, and the point of the gate is only that the geometry and solver are sound. It was previously carrying an asserted target value, which has been removed — a gate must report, not assert.

The surprise was speed. The flux solve takes 1 second where a higher-order discretisation of the same problem took 729. That is a factor of ~700, not the ~40 previously estimated, and it makes a fallback architecture that keeps the reference solver in the loop far more viable than budgeted.

What remains

The physics chain is validated as far as a single fuel channel. Nothing downstream of it exists yet — and the single most safety-relevant feature of this reactor is still absent from the model entirely.

  1. A6Graphite-tipped control rods The rods that scrammed Chernobyl had graphite displacers below the boron absorber. Driving them down from fully withdrawn pushed graphite — a moderator — into a column of water at the bottom of the core, adding reactivity for the first several seconds of the shutdown. The model currently has no absorber material and nowhere for a rod to go: every cell is fuel or graphite. Gate: inserting the displacer alone from below gives positive reactivity. This is a stronger result than the void coefficient because it does not depend on the disputed magnitude.
  2. A7Steady coupled core at 3200 MW Close the loop between neutronics and thermal-hydraulics: power shape sets coolant boiling, boiling sets density, density feeds back into reactivity. Apply the void bracket here, as one visible multiplier on the void-induced term. Gate: critical at full power with a physical axial power shape and channel outlet conditions near 284 °C at ~14 % steam quality. First output the display can show.
  3. A8The AZ-5 transient, run twice Start from the accident state — high burnup, xenon-poisoned, almost all rods withdrawn, low coolant flow — and insert the rods at their real 0.4 m/s. Run it under both ends of the void bracket. Gate: a prompt power rise before the absorber takes over. The deliverable is the comparison: if both variants reach fuel failure at similar times the factor of two is not load-bearing and can stay open. If one runs away and the other does not, closing it becomes the priority.
  4. A9In-core detectors Read the transient through the instruments the operators actually had, including the response lag of self-powered neutron detectors — itself historically significant, since indicated power lagged real power. Gate: traces respond to a local rod insertion, not just to global power.
  5. BReal-time solver The reference codes are single-threaded with no GPU path. A standalone 2-group solver with delayed-precursor tracking and per-channel thermal-hydraulics, validated against the A8 trace rather than written and hoped for. Gate: reproduces the reference transient to a stated tolerance through the excursion, not just at steady state.
  6. CThe mnemonic panel 1661 channel outlet temperatures, 211 rod positions, detector readings — the control-room display as it existed, driven live by the solver. Gate: updates at 10 Hz or better with the solver in the loop.
Open

Known gaps

  • The factor of two is unresolved. Every candidate tested so far has been a nuclear data or library option. Spatial discretisation and self-shielding geometry — which all bite harder in a voided channel, the signature this disagreement has — have never been tested. The highest-information test is a direct comparison of the neutron energy spectrum between the two codes, which would localise the cause in energy rather than guess at it.
  • Xenon is not yet an axis. The database holds equilibrium xenon only. The operating margin story before the accident is a transient-xenon story, so this has to be added.
  • The second database the plan called for cannot be built. DRAGON has no cross-section scaling facility anywhere in its write path. The bracket is carried as a ratio table and applied in the consuming deck instead, which keeps a single source of truth for the physics. Not yet implemented or validated.
  • Unsourced geometry. Pressure tube wall thickness, the two fuel-ring radii and the carrier rod wall are inferred rather than taken from a primary source. The tube alone moves coolant area by 10 %, directly on the disputed quantity.
  • Steam uses liquid-water scattering data in both codes. A shared approximation, so it cancels in the comparison and is wrong in both.
  • Transient thermal-hydraulics is homogeneous-flow only. The drift-flux model is available at steady state but hardcoded off in the transient path. For a positive-void reactor that is a real caveat, and the error needs bounding.
