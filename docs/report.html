For several days the two codes disagreed by about 2× on the void coefficient, and it was not clear which was wrong. It is now closed. Converging DRAGON against itself — a finer energy mesh through the uranium resonances (172 → SHEM-361), zirconium self-shielding, a finer spatial mesh, and characteristics with anisotropic scattering in place of collision probabilities — moved DRAGON most of the way to OpenMC without touching OpenMC at all.

At the burnup Chernobyl Unit 4 actually had, complete voiding is +2.4 to +2.75 $ in every converged variant of both codes. The spread fell from 38 % to 13 %, and the historical outcome was never an input to any of it. Our OpenMC also reproduces the 1990s MCNP and MCU results on a published RBMK benchmark cell, so there is a third-party anchor.

Everything on this page was re-measured on the converged library. The superseded figures — the ±2× bracket, the “+1957 pcm at 20 MWd/t” claim and the 24 September dollars table — are retained only in the project log.

Orientation

Reading the numbers

Everything below is a statement about reactivity — how far a reactor is from steady self-sustaining operation, and in which direction. Three units carry that, and they appear on every chart.

k
The multiplication factor: neutrons in one fission generation divided by neutrons in the one before. k = 1 is steady power. k∞ ("k-infinity") is that ratio for a single fuel cell with no leakage out of the sides; keff is the real, finite reactor.
ρ, pcm
Reactivity, ρ = 1 − 1/k, measured in pcm — pour cent mille, parts per hundred thousand. 100 pcm = 0.1 % change in k. A change in reactivity is always quoted relative to some reference state; here that reference is always the same fuel at the same burnup with the coolant at its normal density.
β
The delayed-neutron fraction. Roughly 0.5 % of fission neutrons are not emitted promptly but seconds to minutes later, by the decay of fission products. Those stragglers are the entire reason a reactor can be controlled by mechanical rods: they stretch the response time from microseconds to seconds.
$ (dollar)
Reactivity divided by β. One dollar is the threshold at which a reactor is critical on prompt neutrons alone — the delayed ones are no longer needed, the stretched response time collapses, and power runs away in milliseconds. Below $1 a reactor is controllable; above it, nothing mechanical is fast enough. This is the number that matters, and it is why β had to be measured rather than assumed.

Two more terms recur:

void coefficient
How reactivity changes as coolant water boils away. In most reactors water is the moderator, so losing it shuts the reaction down — the coefficient is negative and the reactor is self-stabilising. In an RBMK the moderator is graphite (89.6 % of the cell by volume); the water is mainly a neutron absorber. Boiling it off therefore adds reactivity. That positive coefficient is the physics at the centre of this project.
burnup
How much energy a unit mass of fuel has produced, in megawatt-days per tonne (MWd/t). It stands in for how far the fuel's composition has evolved: U-235 consumed, plutonium bred in, fission products accumulated. Chernobyl's core was at high burnup, and nearly every dangerous property below gets worse with it.
A3 · foundation

One bad isotope label

The lattice cell describes one fuel channel: 18 fuel rods in two rings, a zirconium pressure tube, and the graphite block around it. Every material composition for the whole project comes from one shared procedure, so the cell deck and the branch deck cannot drift apart.

That procedure declared the coolant's hydrogen as H1 — free-gas hydrogen, an unbound proton. Hydrogen in liquid water is not free: it is bound into a molecule whose vibrational and rotational modes change how it scatters slow neutrons. The correct label is H1_H2O.

Free-gas hydrogen overstates water's moderating power, which makes the water look more useful than it is — and therefore makes boiling it away look less rewarding. The fix changed k∞ by only 264 pcm, but it changed the full-void worth from +82 pcm to +222 pcm: it nearly tripled the quantity the whole project exists to get right.

processA fix that lives in a copy of the file is not a fix

The correction had in fact been made a day earlier — in a copy of the procedure, RbmkLibH.c2m. Six diagnostic decks were pointed at the copy and reported the right answer, while the two headline decks still called the original and ran the wrong composition. The copy has been deleted and the callers repointed. This is now a standing rule in the project log.

A5b · the structural gap

Connecting the cell to the core

Until now nothing in the project had ever written cross sections to disk. Every good cell calculation left its result in memory and discarded it at exit, so the whole-core solver ran on generic textbook constants for a pressurised-water reactor — a different reactor with the opposite sign of void coefficient. The lattice work and the core work were not connected at all.

MULTICOMPO
A reactivity database. The detailed transport calculation is run once per combination of conditions, condensed from 361 neutron energy groups down to 2, and tabulated. The core solver then interpolates it instead of re-running transport — which it could never afford to do 1661 times per time step.

The database now spans four axes — burnup, coolant density, fuel temperature and graphite temperature — at every combination: 840 points, 14.7 hours, 152 MB. Each point is a full re-solve including re-computed self-shielding, which is not optional: boiling the coolant hardens the neutron spectrum into exactly the uranium resonances that self-shielding governs.

But "a database exists on disk" is not evidence of anything. Three checks:

840branch points, all present, no non-convergence
0.9 pcmworst round-trip loss, 8 nodes spanning every axis extreme
24 pcmworst off-grid interpolation error — what the transient will actually use
−0.5 %β against an independent Monte Carlo measurement

The round trip compares the detailed 361-group transport answer against what comes back out of the database after condensation, homogenisation, storage and interpolation. Losing 0.9 pcm through all of that is three orders of magnitude below the physics uncertainties here. The off-grid test matters more: the transient will never land exactly on a tabulated point, so it measures error at the midpoints of the coolant-density and temperature axes. Burnup is deliberately left on grid nodes — fuel composition does not change over the forty seconds of an accident, while the other three swing violently.

An earlier check on a single corner point had reported 0.4 pcm. That was the best case by a factor of sixteen; the honest number is 6.4.

A5b · delayed neutrons

β falls as the fuel ages

The project had been assuming β = 0.0048 throughout. β is not a constant: it is a property of which nuclide is fissioning. Uranium-235 emits delayed neutrons at 0.0065 of its fissions; the plutonium-239 that breeds in as fuel ages emits them at only 0.0023. As plutonium takes over, β falls.

Both figures were read straight out of the nuclear data library rather than quoted — and the resulting curve drops 31 % over the fuel's life.

The cross-check is worth stating carefully, because the two methods share almost nothing. DRAGON computes a flux-weighted ratio of delayed to total fission production in 361 energy groups on a deterministic transport solve. OpenMC runs the same cell twice in continuous energy by Monte Carlo — once with delayed neutrons enabled and once without — and takes βeff = 1 − kprompt/ktotal. They agree to 0.5 %, inside the Monte Carlo error bar.

near missA wrong answer that was self-consistent

The first extraction returned 0.006524 at every burnup, every void fraction and every temperature — identical to six digits. Two independent mistakes produced it: the database query GETVAL 'NUSIGF' 1 takes a start index, not a count, and the aged fuel's data is stored as 18 separate fissioning nuclides. So element 1 was uranium-235 in both the numerator and the denominator, at every state — and 0.006524 is exactly uranium-235's β.

Being self-consistent across ten different states made it look more trustworthy, not less. What caught it was knowing beforehand what the physics had to do: β must fall, so a flat curve is a defect to explain, not a result to record.

A5a · the central quantity

The void coefficient, and how the disagreement closed

Two independent codes compute the same quantity: how much reactivity is added when the coolant in a fuel channel boils away completely. Both agree it is positive and that it rises steeply with burnup. That is the RBMK's defining hazard reproduced from first principles, by two methods, with no tuning.

They used to disagree on the magnitude by about a factor of two. Chasing that disagreement down took a dedicated segment of work, and the answer was not in the nuclear data. A four-factor decomposition put the whole discrepancy in resonance absorption — uranium-238 and zirconium — with everything thermal already in agreement. Four things were wrong with the deterministic calculation, in order of size:

  1. the 172-group energy mesh, too coarse through the U-238 resonances;
  2. zirconium left at infinite dilution instead of self-shielded;
  3. the flat-flux spatial mesh;
  4. isotropic scattering with a transport correction, instead of characteristics with P1.

None of them is an approximation anyone would defend once named; each was simply the default. Fixing the first two in production and carrying the last two as a measured correction is what the three curves below represent.

The three are now a bracket rather than a contradiction: a band roughly ±15 % wide, carried forward explicitly so the accident simulation can be run at both edges and say whether the remaining width matters. That is a different and much more useful object than a factor of two with no structure.

checked against a third partyNot just the two codes agreeing with each other

Two codes converging on each other can converge on the same mistake. On the published RBMK Safety Review Project cell (Alexeev et al., 1998) our OpenMC reproduces the MCNP and MCU results of the 1990s to within a few tenths of a percent on watered k∞, and converged DRAGON comes within 4 % on the void effect where the production settings were 42–48 % low. An independent 2007 study had already found DRAGON 21 % low against MCNP on this reactor, which is the same finding from outside.

Result

Why it mattered that the fuel was old

Converting reactivity to dollars combines the two results above, and the two effects compound: the numerator rises steeply with burnup while β, the denominator, falls. The danger grows twice over.

At the burnup Unit 4 actually had — 10.9 MWd/kgU, not the 20 the project assumed for its first week — complete voiding is worth +1.94 $, +2.41 $ and +2.72 $ across the three variants. Every one of them is above prompt critical, and the whole bracket sits on one side of the line. Earlier, on the unconverged library, it straddled — +0.6 $ against +2.7 $ — and the question of which code to believe was load-bearing. It no longer is.

Two effects compound to get there. The numerator rises steeply with burnup as plutonium builds in; β, the denominator, falls from 0.00682 to 0.00498 over the same range because plutonium-239 yields fewer delayed neutrons than uranium-235. The reactor became more dangerous twice over as its fuel aged, and neither half of that is a modelling choice.

caveatThis is not yet the accident

What is plotted is the equilibrium worth of completely voiding a single channel at fixed burnup — not a transient, and not the whole core, whose void reactivity also depends on leakage, the rod pattern and the axial power shape. Those are exactly the parts still being built. The reference transient will be run at both edges of the bracket.

A2 · whole core

The core geometry solves, and quickly

The 3-D core deck — 49 × 49 × 32 nodes, 12.25 × 12.25 × 8 m, 76 832 regions — now runs and converges, at keff = 1.123062. That value is not a physics result: it still uses the placeholder textbook constants, and the point of the gate is only that the geometry and solver are sound. It was previously carrying an asserted target value, which has been removed — a gate must report, not assert.

The surprise was speed. The flux solve takes about 2 seconds where a higher-order discretisation of the same problem took 729, making a fallback architecture that keeps the reference solver in the loop far more viable than budgeted.

correctedThe fast solver loses a third of the rod worth

That 1-second figure was measured with a mesh-centred finite-difference scheme, chosen for speed before the model had any control rods in it. Once it did, the choice turned out to be untenable: one mesh point per 25 cm node cannot represent the flux depression inside a black absorber, and it under-predicts absorber worth by 31–34 % at every state tested. It also cannot carry the equivalence that makes homogenised rod data valid — the iteration oscillates and never converges.

The fix costs almost nothing, because the alternative that had looked expensive was a much higher-order scheme. The lowest-order mixed-dual discretisation runs the same core in 2.11 s against 1.95 s, and the two agree to 0.7 pcm when no absorber is present — which is itself the control confirming the mechanism.

A6 · the rods, and where the core is

The feature that made the scram dangerous

The rods that scrammed Chernobyl carried graphite displacers below the boron absorber. Driving them down from fully withdrawn pushed graphite — a moderator — into a column of water at the bottom of the core, adding reactivity for the first seconds of the shutdown. Modelling that is the point of this stage, and it is the one result that does not depend on the void-coefficient bracket at all.

The channel itself is now built in both codes from a single shared description, so the two cannot drift apart by transcription, and every section — boron absorber, graphite displacer, water column, drained channel — has been measured against Monte Carlo. Two findings, one good and one not.

The homogenisation is exact; the neighbour data is not. Collapsing the rod cell to two energy groups and solving it with diffusion theory reproduces the transport answer to about 1 pcm — provided the eight fuel cells around it carry data generated in the rod's presence. Give them ordinary infinite-lattice fuel data, which is what a whole-core model must do, and the displacer and drained-channel worths fall 7–10 % short. A fuel cell next to a rod is not a fuel cell in an infinite lattice, and the database has no axis that could know the difference. That is the current blocker, and the fix — an explicit equivalence carried by the rod cell alone — is designed but not yet built.

The deterministic method is 5–12 % low on the section worths even converged, against Monte Carlo. That residual is measured per state and carried as a correction rather than tuned away. It is not diffuse: comparing absolute multiplication factors rather than differences localises it, and it behaves like a spectral effect that shifts with coolant density rather than a single bad number.

A6d · the core map

Two figures, twelve years apart, that check each other

A whole-core model needs to know what sits at every one of the 1888 lattice positions. The project had been placing 1661 fuel channels in a circle, which is wrong in a way no amount of care downstream could repair: the real boundary is a staircase, the rods are interspersed with the fuel rather than ringed outside it, and no centred circle reproduces the count — circle cell counts step by eight.

Two independent sources fixed it. A cartogram of Leningrad-2's initial loading from the reactor's designer, digitised by hand, gives the outline and what sits at every position including the rod types. A figure in the IAEA's INSAG-7 report gives Unit 4's rod positions and insertion depths at 01:22:30 on the night of the accident, digitised by glyph detection.

Registering one onto the other is exact. All 223 rod-lattice sites land on sites of the other map at a single offset, with nothing left over. Two figures, twelve years and two reactors apart, digitised by completely different means — so each is now a check on the other, and neither was adjusted to make them agree.

What fell out of the registration was not just the map. The twelve blank positions in the accident figure turn out to be exactly the twelve axial power-detector channels of the cartogram — an inference that became a measurement. And Unit 4's 211 rods sit on Leningrad-2's 179 rod sites plus 32 more, with no site left to guess at.

1888total positions, every count closing

Five separate counts close against five sources that were not used to build the map. What remains an assumption is not which positions hold rods — that is measured — but what type 32 of the 211 rods are; the choice is recorded per row so the transient study can vary it.

Next

What remains

The physics chain is validated as far as a single fuel channel. Nothing downstream of it exists yet — and the single most safety-relevant feature of this reactor is still absent from the model entirely.

  1. A6Control rods — gate measured The core now solves end to end on real cross sections in every material: 1661 fuel channels, 227 rod and detector positions, and a sourced one-metre graphite reflector. The rods are modelled as they are built — five metres of boron carbide, a 1.25 m water gap, and a 4.5 m graphite displacer below it — and that geometry checks itself: with the rod fully withdrawn the displacer and its two water gaps fill the seven-metre core exactly, to the centimetre, from three lengths quoted separately. Gate result: the displacer is worth +2060 pcm against a water column. The first 1.25 m of rod travel — the displacer alone — is −175 pcm in a core with a centre-peaked axial flux, and turns positive, to +177 pcm, once the flux is skewed toward the bottom; tilt it back up and it goes negative again. So the mechanism is in the model and conditional exactly as the gate words it, and the condition is now a measured criterion rather than a caveat.
  2. A7Steady coupled core at 3200 MW Close the loop between neutronics and thermal-hydraulics: power shape sets coolant boiling, boiling sets density, density feeds back into reactivity. Apply the void bracket here, as one visible multiplier on the void-induced term. Gate: critical at full power with a physical axial power shape and channel outlet conditions near 284 °C at ~14 % steam quality. First output the display can show.
  3. A8The AZ-5 transient, run twice Start from the accident state — high burnup, xenon-poisoned, almost all rods withdrawn, low coolant flow — and insert the rods at their real 0.4 m/s, from the measured 01:22:30 pattern. Run it at both edges of the void bracket. Gate: a prompt power rise before the absorber takes over. The deliverable is the comparison: if both variants reach fuel failure at similar times the remaining 15 % of bracket width is not load-bearing. If one runs away and the other does not, closing it becomes the priority.
  4. A9In-core detectors Read the transient through the instruments the operators actually had, including the response lag of self-powered neutron detectors — itself historically significant, since indicated power lagged real power. Gate: traces respond to a local rod insertion, not just to global power.
  5. BReal-time solver The reference codes are single-threaded with no GPU path. A standalone 2-group solver with delayed-precursor tracking and per-channel thermal-hydraulics, validated against the A8 trace rather than written and hoped for. Gate: reproduces the reference transient to a stated tolerance through the excursion, not just at steady state. Assessed 2026-09-29: build this, rather than porting the reference codes out of Fortran — most of the friction there has been the physics data model, which a rewrite reproduces, and a port starts at zero validation.
  6. CThe mnemonic panel 1661 channel outlet temperatures, 211 rod positions, detector readings — the control-room display as it existed, driven live by the solver. Gate: updates at 10 Hz or better with the solver in the loop.

Known gaps

  • The neutronics has no comparison against measurement. It is verification — two codes, and a third-party benchmark that is itself a code comparison. The one candidate measured core-level reference, a cold zero-power start-up at Smolensk-3, is blocked on a 1993–94 report that is not available online. Until that is found the neutronics is a model checked against other models.
    The thermal-hydraulics now has one. A published single-channel operating point — power, flow, inlet state, exit steam quality, outlet temperature, clad and centreline temperatures — over-determines itself, so the model can be given the first three and asked for the rest. It returns the outlet temperature to 0.2 K and the exit quality to 16 %, which is as close as the published figures come to agreeing with each other: the quoted quality sits outside the range its own quoted power and flow allow. The model closes its own energy balance to 1.2 %.
  • Boron carbide density is in no source we have found. It is carried at 2.0 g/cm³ with a 1.7–2.3 bracket, and it is the largest single open input for rod worth. It will be reported as a band rather than a number.
  • Homogenised rod data is 7–10 % short on the displacer and drained-channel worths once the surrounding fuel carries ordinary infinite-lattice data. The cause is identified and the fix designed; it is the current blocker.
  • The graphite reflector is built, and turns out barely to matter. It was a placeholder; it is now four rings of transport-homogenised graphite out to a stack boundary set from the published column count — whose outer layer independently reproduces the published count of 156 water-cooled reflector channels, exactly. Measured in the core, the whole radial reflector is worth 131 pcm and only 2.6 pcm leaks past it, so the large homogenisation error measured on a small test strip cannot propagate: the entire band the dataset can move core k in is about 130 pcm wide.
  • Burnup is uniform across the core. Every channel sits at the core-average 10.9 MWd/kgU, because no source gives where the fresh and spent fuel actually was. Channel power peaking comes out at 1.90 against a design figure near 1.4, and that gap is the missing burnup flattening. It also matters more than it looks: the positive-displacer result above needs a bottom-skewed axial flux, and burnup placement is one of the two things that produce one.
  • The control-rod stroke is unsourced. A five-metre absorber cannot cover a seven-metre core, so which five metres it covers when fully inserted changes the all-rods-in worth. The displacer results do not depend on it; the full-insertion figure does.
  • Xenon is not yet a database axis. The operating-margin story before the accident is a transient-xenon story, and Unit 4 was heavily poisoned at 01:23.
  • The deterministic method is 5–12 % low on the control-rod section worths even converged. Measured per state and carried as a correction, not tuned away, but not closed.
  • Steam uses liquid-water scattering data in both codes. A shared approximation, so it cancels in the comparison and is wrong in both.
  • Transient thermal-hydraulics is homogeneous-flow only. The drift-flux model is available at steady state but hardcoded off in the transient path. For a positive-void reactor that is a real caveat, and the error needs bounding. The consequence has been taken: the steady coupled core will be converged with drift flux off, so that the void model does not change discontinuously the moment the transient starts.
  • Rod types for 32 of the 211 positions are assumed, by elimination from published second-stage counts. Recorded per position so the transient can vary them.
  • The pellet–clad gap conductance is in no source we have, and it is what the fuel centreline temperature hangs on — 1300 °C or 2000 °C depending on it. Two independent routes put it near 2 kW/m²/K: bare helium conduction across the measured 100 µm gap, and inverting the published centreline temperature. The solver's own correlation assumes a far tighter gap and is the outlier, so the value is now set explicitly rather than left to it.
  • Nothing computes the graphite temperature. The cross-section database has an axis for it — it is a large effect in a graphite-moderated reactor — but the value is pinned at a constant 750 K everywhere, because the solver has no conduction model for the moderator. It has to be supplied from outside.
