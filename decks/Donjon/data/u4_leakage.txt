READING IT.

The radial reflector is worth +131.3 pcm in this core, and only
+2.6 pcm leaks past it -- 1 m of graphite is already an almost
perfect reflector.  A MIRROR at the core edge is worth +1111.5 pcm, which
is the whole radial leakage.  Those are not in conflict: a mirror sets
B_r^2 = 0, while a real reflector only extends the effective radius.
Diffusion theory with D_r = 0.84, L_r = 54 cm gives a reflector saving
of 49 cm on a 590 cm radius; with M^2 = 657 cm^2 read off the
mirror-at-core-edge row, that predicts ~155 pcm against the measured
+131.3.  The model is behaving.

THIS CLOSES THE PHASE 1b OPEN QUESTION.  The band between having no
radial reflector at all (-131.3 pcm) and a perfect mirror at the stack
edge (+2.6 pcm) is the whole room the reflector dataset has to move
core k in, and it is about 130 pcm wide.  The 7152 pcm measured on the
4-fuel/4-reflector strip therefore does not propagate to the core: that
test over-weighted the reflector by two orders of magnitude.

Measured, not argued.  Collapsing all four rings onto the single
dataset nearest the core moves k by +3.9 pcm, so the depth
resolution the reflector COMPO provides is worth almost nothing here.
Putting the cooled ring in the wrong place moves it by -90.6 and
-88.0 pcm.  WHERE THE WATER IS matters; the spectrum depth does not.

What this does NOT bound: a dataset that got graphite ABSORPTION badly
wrong would act as a sink rather than a reflector and could fall below
the no-reflector row, outside the band above.  The rows here bound
datasets built the way ours was, not arbitrary ones.
