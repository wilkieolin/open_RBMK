READING IT.

THE TWO FLOW CASES AGREE TO 1 pcm, AND THAT IS THE CHECK ON THE PINNING.
   Valving every channel to its own power and giving every channel the
   same velocity are very different coolant states, but with the
   density feedback pinned the only path from flow to k is cut, so the
   two must give the same eigenvalue -- and they do, -240 against
   -241 pcm.  Had they differed, the pin would have been leaking.
   It also means these runs say nothing about the flow distribution;
   that question reopens when the DCA axis reaches the inlet.

The loop converges, and it converges geometrically -- the fuel
temperature error falls by a steady factor each pass and the
eigenvalue with it.  The coupling machinery is therefore right: NCR:
reads the thermal-hydraulic state back out of the fuel map, MACINI:
and TRIVAA: rebuild on it, and FLPOW: feeds THM: in turn.

WHAT STOPS IT IS THE CROSS-SECTION TABLE, NOT THE SOLVER.  The A5b
MULTICOMPO was built around a lattice-cell nominal state -- coolant
0.72 g/cm3, fuel 600-2000 K -- and a real core leaves that box at both
ends.  The inlet is subcooled at 0.778 g/cm3, above the DCA axis; the
ends of a low-power channel sit near coolant temperature, below the TF
axis.  Both are the operating state falling outside the table, and
both are one more value on an existing axis.

PRICED, so the extension is a decision and not a reflex: pinning the
coolant density at 0.72 core-wide costs 103 pcm (dk/dDCA = -0.01779
from the 0.60 and 0.72 grid points, extrapolated 0.058 past the axis
end), and that is an upper bound because only the subcooled lower part
of each channel is above 0.72.  The DCA axis has 7 values, so one more
is 1/7 of the branch tensor on a rebuild A6b already needs for xenon.
